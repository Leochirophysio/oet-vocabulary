acrocyanosis
/ˌæk.rəʊ.saɪ.əˈnəʊ.sɪs/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Signs & Symptoms 心肺 Cardiopulmonary
Medical Definition
A benign condition characterised by persistent bluish-purple discolouration of the hands and feet, commonly observed in neonates immediately after birth; caused by vasomotor instability and reduced peripheral perfusion rather than central hypoxia.
繁體中文
肢端發紺
Common Collocations
neonatal acrocyanosis benign acrocyanosis peripheral acrocyanosis persistent acrocyanosis acrocyanosis at birth
OET Clinical Example
"The neonate displayed transient acrocyanosis of the hands and feet immediately following delivery; given the absence of central cyanosis and a normal oxygen saturation on pulse oximetry, no intervention was required and the finding resolved within 48 hours."
Related Words
cyanosis (central — more serious) peripheral cyanosis (synonym) acral cyanosis (synonym) vasomotor instability perfusion (blood flow)
Word 2 of 5
surfactant
/sɜːˈfæk.tənt/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
心肺 Cardiopulmonary Procedures & Interventions
Medical Definition
A complex mixture of phospholipids and proteins produced by type II alveolar cells that reduces surface tension within the alveoli, preventing their collapse on exhalation; deficiency of surfactant in premature infants leads to respiratory distress syndrome (RDS).
繁體中文
表面活性劑（肺泡表面活性物質）
Common Collocations
surfactant deficiency exogenous surfactant surfactant replacement therapy pulmonary surfactant surfactant administration
OET Clinical Example
"The premature infant born at 28 weeks gestation received exogenous surfactant replacement therapy via endotracheal instillation within two hours of birth, resulting in a significant improvement in oxygenation and a reduction in ventilator requirements."
Related Words
phospholipid (component) atelectasis (collapse — antonym effect) respiratory distress syndrome / RDS alveoli (air sacs) prematurity (cause of deficiency)

obtundation
/ˌɒb.tʌnˈdeɪ.ʃən/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
神經 Neurological Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
A state of reduced consciousness in which the patient is less alert and responsive than normal; the patient is difficult to arouse but responds to stimulation. More severe than lethargy but less severe than stupor or coma.
繁體中文
意識鈍化；反應遲鈍
Common Collocations
mild obtundation moderate obtundation obtundation on presentation obtundation following head injury signs of obtundation
OET Clinical Example
"On arrival to the emergency department, the 7-year-old male demonstrated obtundation with a Glasgow Coma Scale score of 11 following a reported fall from a height; neurological observations were commenced hourly and urgent CT imaging was arranged."
Related Words
lethargy (less severe — drowsiness) stupor (more severe — minimal response) coma (most severe — unresponsive) level of consciousness / LOC GCS (Glasgow Coma Scale)

amblyopia
/ˌæm.bliˈəʊ.pi.ə/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
神經 Neurological Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Reduced visual acuity in one or both eyes that is not fully correctable with spectacles alone; commonly called "lazy eye," it results from abnormal visual development in early childhood due to strabismus, significant refractive error, or visual deprivation.
繁體中文
弱視（懶惰眼）
Common Collocations
strabismic amblyopia refractive amblyopia amblyopia treatment amblyopia screening deprivation amblyopia
OET Clinical Example
"The 4-year-old female was referred to paediatric ophthalmology following detection of left amblyopia at routine vision screening; spectacle correction and occlusion patching therapy have been prescribed to stimulate visual development in the affected eye."
Related Words
lazy eye (lay term) strabismus (squint — common cause) visual acuity (sharpness of vision) anisometropia (unequal refractive error) amblyopic (adj)

urticaria
/ˌɜː.tɪˈkeə.ri.ə/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical

Medical Definition
A skin condition characterised by raised, erythematous, intensely pruritic wheals (hives) that appear suddenly on the skin as a result of an allergic or non-allergic reaction; individual wheals typically resolve within 24 hours, though the condition may recur.
繁體中文

OET Clinical Example

Related Words
hives (lay term) angioedema (deeper tissue swelling) pruritus (itching) anaphylaxis (severe allergic reaction) urticarial (adj)
✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 102)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Baby Mei, a female neonate born at 30 weeks gestation via emergency caesarean section, was admitted to the neonatal intensive care unit following delivery.

At birth, the infant displayed acrocyanosis of the extremities, with preserved central colour and an oxygen saturation of 95% on room air. Central cyanosis was excluded. She was noted to have laboured respirations consistent with surfactant deficiency, and exogenous surfactant replacement therapy was administered via endotracheal tube within the first hour of life, with subsequent improvement in her respiratory status and ventilator weaning commenced over the following 48 hours.

On day 5 of admission, nursing staff reported a period of obtundation lasting approximately 20 minutes, during which the infant was difficult to rouse. Urgent cranial ultrasound was performed to exclude intraventricular haemorrhage. Following stabilisation, a physiotherapy referral was made to address positioning, developmental handling, and respiratory support during the ongoing admission.

The family was counselled regarding the infant's prognosis and the importance of early developmental follow-up, including paediatric ophthalmology review to screen for amblyopia and retinopathy of prematurity as she approaches term-corrected age.

📌 OET Writing Tip: In paediatric referral letters, always document the corrected gestational age, specify central vs peripheral findings (e.g. central cyanosis vs acrocyanosis), and use precise clinical timelines (e.g. "within the first hour of life," "day 5 of admission"). Avoid ambiguous phrases such as "the baby was a bit blue" — use graded, specific clinical language.
