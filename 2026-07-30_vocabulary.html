analgesia

Medical Definition

Medical Definition

Word 3 of 5
thromboprophylaxis
/ˌθrɒm.bəʊ.prəˈfɪ.læk.sɪs/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Procedures & Interventions
Medical Definition
Preventive pharmacological or mechanical measures taken to reduce the risk of blood clot (thrombus) formation in patients at increased risk, such as those post-surgery or with prolonged immobility.
繁體中文
血栓預防治療
Common Collocations
mechanical thromboprophylaxis pharmacological thromboprophylaxis thromboprophylaxis protocol adequate thromboprophylaxis thromboprophylaxis regimen
OET Clinical Example
"As part of the post-operative thromboprophylaxis protocol, the patient was commenced on active ankle exercises and fitted with graduated compression stockings, alongside low-molecular-weight heparin prescribed by the surgical team."
Related Words
thrombus (blood clot) thrombosis (clot formation) prophylaxis (prevention) anticoagulation (drug treatment) deep vein thrombosis (DVT)

oscillation
/ˌɒs.ɪˈleɪ.ʃən/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Procedures & Interventions 心肺 Cardiopulmonary
Medical Definition
A repetitive back-and-forth or vibratory movement; in respiratory physiotherapy, high-frequency chest wall oscillation generates vibrations to loosen and mobilise mucus secretions, facilitating airway clearance.
繁體中文
振盪；震動（胸腔物理治療技術）
Common Collocations

OET Clinical Example
"High-frequency chest wall oscillation was initiated using a vest device to assist with secretion clearance in this patient with cystic fibrosis, as standard manual airway clearance techniques had proven insufficient in mobilising tenacious lower lobe secretions."
Related Words
oscillate (verb) oscillatory (adj) vibration (manual technique) autogenic drainage (related airway technique) airway clearance
✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 83)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Mr. Dawit Bekele, a 61-year-old male with a known background of coronary artery disease, was admitted to the cardiothoracic ward following elective coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG). He is referred to the physiotherapy department on post-operative day two for respiratory management and early mobility.

On initial assessment, Mr. Bekele reported significant incisional pain at the sternal wound site, limiting his ability to cough effectively. Adequate analgesia was administered by the nursing team thirty minutes prior to the physiotherapy session, which subsequently enabled satisfactory chest expansion and productive coughing with minimal splinting required.

High-frequency chest wall oscillation was applied bilaterally to assist in mobilising secretions from the lower lobes, where coarse crackles had been noted on auscultation. The medical team confirmed that left ventricular function remained acceptable for graded physical activity, with an ejection fraction of 42% on the most recent echocardiogram.

Mechanical thromboprophylaxis — including bilateral graduated compression stockings and active ankle pumping exercises — was reinforced during the session. Mr. Bekele was subsequently assisted to sit out of bed and ambulate five metres with a physiotherapist providing close supervision. Discharge home is anticipated within four to five days, at which point he will be referred to the outpatient coronary rehabilitation programme.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When documenting cardiac rehabilitation referrals, always include current ventricular function / ejection fraction, operative procedure and date, and current functional status. Specify whether thromboprophylaxis is mechanical, pharmacological, or both — this informs the receiving clinician's safety planning.
