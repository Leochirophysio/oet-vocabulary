Word 1 of 5
hamstring
/ˈhæm.strɪŋ/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
骨科 Musculoskeletal
Medical Definition
Any of the group of three muscles (biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus) located at the posterior aspect of the thigh; collectively responsible for knee flexion and hip extension. Also used as an adjective (e.g. hamstring tendon) or verb meaning to severe these tendons, rendering the limb lame.
繁體中文
腿後腱肌群；膕旁肌
Common Collocations
hamstring strain hamstring tear hamstring tightness hamstring flexibility hamstring rehabilitation
OET Clinical Example
"Mr. Tane Parata, a 28-year-old rugby player, sustained a grade II left hamstring strain during a competitive match and was referred for physiotherapy management including progressive eccentric loading and return-to-sport programming."
Related Words
biceps femoris (lateral hamstring) semitendinosus semimembranosus posterior thigh (anatomical region) quadriceps (antagonist group)

osteochondral
/ˌɒs.ti.əʊˈkɒn.drəl/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
骨科 Musculoskeletal Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Relating to or involving both bone (osteo-) and cartilage (chondral). Commonly used to describe defects, lesions, or injuries affecting the articular cartilage and the underlying subchondral bone simultaneously.
繁體中文
骨軟骨的
Common Collocations

OET Clinical Example
"MRI confirmed an osteochondral lesion at the medial talar dome, graded III, for which the patient underwent arthroscopic microfracture prior to referral for post-operative physiotherapy rehabilitation."
Related Words
cartilage (articular) subchondral bone chondromalacia (cartilage softening) osteochondritis dissecans articular surface
Word 3 of 5
subchondral
/sʌbˈkɒn.drəl/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
骨科 Musculoskeletal Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Situated or occurring beneath (sub-) the cartilage (chondral), referring to the layer of bone immediately underlying the articular cartilage of a joint. Subchondral changes are commonly seen on imaging in degenerative joint disease.
繁體中文
軟骨下的
Common Collocations
subchondral bone subchondral sclerosis subchondral oedema subchondral cyst subchondral plate
OET Clinical Example
"Radiological review demonstrated significant subchondral sclerosis and cyst formation at the medial compartment of the left knee, consistent with advanced osteoarthritis; the patient was referred for conservative physiotherapy management pending surgical review."
Related Words
osteochondral (adj — involving cartilage and bone) sclerosis (hardening of bone) osteophyte (bony spur) articular cartilage (overlying layer) bone marrow oedema
Word 4 of 5
nocturnal
/nɒkˈtɜː.nəl/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Signs & Symptoms 骨科 Musculoskeletal
Medical Definition
Occurring during or relating to the night. In clinical practice, nocturnal symptoms (particularly pain that disturbs sleep or wakes a patient) are considered a red flag in musculoskeletal assessment, as they may indicate inflammatory, neoplastic, or serious pathology rather than mechanical causes.
繁體中文
夜間的；夜晚發生的
Common Collocations
nocturnal pain nocturnal symptoms nocturnal cramps nocturnal dyspnoea nocturnal waking
OET Clinical Example
"The patient reported persistent nocturnal shoulder pain that regularly disturbed sleep; given the presence of this red flag alongside unexplained weight loss, urgent oncological screening was arranged prior to physiotherapy assessment."
Related Words
nocturia (urination at night — related noun) diurnal (antonym — occurring during the day) nocturnal enuresis (bed-wetting) red flag (clinical warning sign) sleep disturbance
Word 5 of 5
dysaesthesia
/ˌdɪs.ɪsˈθiː.zi.ə/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Signs & Symptoms 神經 Neurological
Medical Definition
An abnormal, unpleasant sensation — such as burning, tingling, electric shock-like feeling, or pain — that occurs either spontaneously or in response to normally non-painful stimuli. Distinct from paraesthesia (which is abnormal but not necessarily unpleasant) in that dysaesthesia is inherently distressing to the patient.
繁體中文
感覺障礙；異常感覺（令人不適）
Common Collocations
painful dysaesthesia dysaesthesia of the lower limbs burning dysaesthesia central dysaesthesia dysaesthesia following nerve injury
OET Clinical Example
"The patient described a persistent burning dysaesthesia along the lateral aspect of the right lower limb following L4/5 discectomy, which was significantly limiting her participation in rehabilitation and warranted review by the neurosurgical team."
Related Words
paraesthesia (abnormal sensation, not necessarily unpleasant) hyperalgesia (increased pain sensitivity) allodynia (pain from non-painful stimulus) neuropathy (nerve dysfunction) nociception (pain detection)
✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 82)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Mr. James Tuhoe, a 34-year-old semi-professional rugby league player, was referred to the outpatient physiotherapy department following right knee arthroscopy for repair of a grade III osteochondral defect at the medial femoral condyle, identified alongside a concurrent hamstring avulsion at the proximal musculotendinous junction.

Pre-operatively, the patient reported nocturnal knee pain rated 7/10 on the Numeric Pain Rating Scale, which consistently disrupted sleep and had failed to respond to conservative analgesia over a six-week period. Neurological screening during the initial assessment revealed a burning dysaesthesia along the posterolateral thigh, attributed to local nerve irritation from haematoma formation at the hamstring injury site.

Post-operative imaging confirmed successful repair of the osteochondral defect with evidence of early subchondral bone remodelling. Physiotherapy aims include progressive weight-bearing, quadriceps and hamstring strengthening, neuromuscular re-education, and graduated return to sport over a twelve-week programme.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When documenting neurological symptoms such as dysaesthesia, always specify the quality (burning, electric, stabbing), distribution (dermatomal or peripheral nerve territory), and impact on function (sleep, rehabilitation participation). This level of detail demonstrates clinical reasoning and is expected in OET Band B+ writing responses.
