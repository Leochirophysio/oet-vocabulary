intussusception
/ˌɪn.tə.sʌsˈep.ʃən/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
小兒 Paediatric Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
A condition in which one segment of the intestine telescopes or folds inward into an adjacent segment, causing bowel obstruction; the most common cause of acute intestinal obstruction in infants aged 6–36 months, most frequently affecting the ileocolic junction.
繁體中文
腸套疊
Common Collocations
ileocolic intussusception pneumatic reduction of intussusception intussusception recurrence spontaneous reduction surgical management of intussusception
OET Clinical Example
"The 9-month-old male presented with a 6-hour history of episodic colicky abdominal pain and passage of blood-stained mucous stool; abdominal ultrasound confirmed ileocolic intussusception, and he was transferred to the paediatric surgery unit for pneumatic reduction under fluoroscopic guidance."
Related Words
intussuscipiens (outer segment) intussusceptum (inner segment) bowel obstruction volvulus (differential) pneumatic reduction (procedure)

strabismus
/strəˈbɪz.məs/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
小兒 Paediatric Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
A condition in which the two eyes are misaligned and do not point in the same direction simultaneously; commonly referred to as a squint; may be convergent (esotropia), divergent (exotropia), or vertical; if untreated in childhood, may lead to amblyopia (lazy eye).
繁體中文
斜視
Common Collocations
convergent strabismus divergent strabismus intermittent strabismus congenital strabismus surgical correction of strabismus
OET Clinical Example
"During the 18-month developmental review, persistent convergent strabismus was noted in the left eye; the family was advised to seek urgent ophthalmological assessment to prevent the development of amblyopia."
Related Words
esotropia (inward turn) exotropia (outward turn) amblyopia (lazy eye — consequence) squint (lay term) ocular palsy (differential)

rhinorrhoea
/ˌraɪ.nəˈrɪ.ə/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Excessive or persistent discharge of mucus from the nose; commonly called a 'runny nose'; may be associated with viral upper respiratory tract infection, allergic rhinitis, foreign body, or — in rare cases — a cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) leak following head trauma.

OET Clinical Example
"The infant presented with a 4-day history of profuse clear rhinorrhoea, low-grade pyrexia, and mild respiratory distress; examination was consistent with viral upper respiratory tract infection, and parents were advised regarding symptomatic management."
Related Words
rhinitis (nasal inflammation) coryza (acute rhinitis — synonym) nasal discharge (lay term) nasal congestion (related symptom) rhinorrhoeal (adj)

hepatosplenomegaly
/ˌhep.ə.tə.ˌspliː.nəʊˈmeg.ə.li/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Signs & Symptoms 小兒 Paediatric
Medical Definition
Simultaneous enlargement of both the liver and the spleen beyond their normal sizes; a clinical sign detected on abdominal palpation that may indicate systemic infection, haematological disorders, metabolic disease, or malignancy; particularly significant in paediatric assessment.
繁體中文
肝脾腫大
Common Collocations

OET Clinical Example
"On abdominal examination, moderate hepatosplenomegaly was identified, with the liver palpable 3 cm below the right costal margin and the spleen tip palpable on inspiration; these findings were consistent with Epstein-Barr virus infection and haematological review was requested."
Related Words
hepatomegaly (liver enlargement only) splenomegaly (spleen enlargement only) organomegaly (organ enlargement — broader) hepatosplenic (adj) abdominal palpation (assessment method)
Word 5 of 5
enuresis
/ˌen.jʊˈriː.sɪs/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
小兒 Paediatric Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Involuntary urination beyond the developmental age at which voluntary bladder control is expected; classified as nocturnal (bedwetting during sleep), diurnal (daytime wetting), or combined; further categorised as primary (continence never achieved) or secondary (wetting following at least six months of dryness).
繁體中文
遺尿症；尿床
Common Collocations
nocturnal enuresis primary enuresis secondary enuresis monosymptomatic enuresis management of enuresis
OET Clinical Example
"James, a 6-year-old male, was referred to the paediatric continence physiotherapy service for assessment and management of primary nocturnal enuresis; his parents reported bedwetting on five to six nights per week despite appropriate fluid management and consistent toileting routines."
Related Words
bedwetting (lay term) urinary incontinence (broader term) nocturia (night voiding in adults) enuretic (adj) bladder alarm therapy (treatment)
✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 81)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Master Liam Chen, a 4-year-old male, was referred by his paediatrician to the continence physiotherapy service for assessment and management of primary nocturnal enuresis. His parents reported that he had never achieved consistent night-time dryness, experiencing bedwetting on four to five nights per week despite appropriate fluid restriction in the evening and a regular toileting routine before bed.

Background history was notable for hospital admission at 8 months of age for ileocolic intussusception, which was successfully managed by pneumatic reduction without surgical complication. He was otherwise meeting developmental milestones and had no neurological concerns on file.

At the initial physiotherapy appointment, Liam presented with bilateral clear rhinorrhoea and nasal congestion, consistent with a concurrent viral upper respiratory tract infection of two days' duration. Incidentally, on abdominal examination, mild hepatosplenomegaly was detected; the parents were advised to follow up with their GP regarding this finding prior to the next appointment. Additionally, persistent convergent strabismus was noted in the right eye, and ophthalmological review was recommended to exclude amblyopia.

Physiotherapy assessment identified a pattern consistent with primary monosymptomatic nocturnal enuresis. A bladder diary was commenced and enuresis alarm therapy was initiated, with education provided to both Liam and his parents regarding expected outcomes and adherence strategies.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When documenting paediatric referrals, always specify the child's age, clarify whether enuresis is primary or secondary, note incidental findings alongside the main complaint, and record any recommended follow-up actions clearly. Avoid ambiguous terms — use "bilateral clear rhinorrhoea" not "runny nose," and "ileocolic intussusception" not simply "bowel problem."
