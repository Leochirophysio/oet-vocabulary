festination
/ˌfes.tɪˈneɪ.ʃən/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
神經 Neurological Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
An involuntary tendency to take short, rapid, shuffling steps at an increasingly accelerating pace; a characteristic gait abnormality seen in Parkinson's disease and other basal ganglia disorders, often associated with postural instability and a significant falls risk.
繁體中文
前衝步態（慌張步態）
Common Collocations
festinating gait progressive festination festination on turning festination in Parkinson's disease gait festination
OET Clinical Example
"Mr. Chen demonstrated marked festination during gait assessment, with an increasingly rapid but shortened stride length and forward trunk lean, placing him at significant risk of falls without close supervision."
Related Words
festinating (adjective) propulsive gait (synonym) retropulsion (backward variant) Parkinson's disease (primary context) freezing of gait (related)

lability
/ləˈbɪl.ɪ.ti/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
神經 Neurological Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
The tendency to undergo rapid, unexpected, and exaggerated changes in mood or emotional state; in neurology, emotional lability refers to pathological and disproportionate laughing or crying that is out of keeping with the patient's underlying mood, commonly seen following stroke, traumatic brain injury, or dementia.
繁體中文
情緒不穩定（情緒易變性）
Common Collocations
emotional lability mood lability affective lability post-stroke lability labile affect
OET Clinical Example
"Following her CVA, Mrs. Park exhibited significant emotional lability, with episodes of unprovoked crying that were distressing to both the patient and her family; referral to neuropsychology was arranged to support ongoing management."
Related Words
labile (adjective) pseudobulbar affect (specific condition) affective dysregulation (related) emotional incontinence (synonym) mood instability (lay term)
Word 3 of 5
supraspinatus
/ˌsuː.prəˈspaɪ.nə.təs/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
骨科 Musculoskeletal
Medical Definition
A rotator cuff muscle of the shoulder arising from the supraspinous fossa of the scapula and inserting into the greater tubercle of the humerus; it initiates the first 15–30 degrees of shoulder abduction and plays a key role in stabilising the glenohumeral joint. It is the most commonly injured rotator cuff structure.
繁體中文
棘上肌
Common Collocations
supraspinatus tear supraspinatus tendinopathy supraspinatus impingement partial-thickness supraspinatus tear full-thickness supraspinatus rupture
OET Clinical Example
"MRI of the right shoulder revealed a partial-thickness supraspinatus tear at the musculotendinous junction, consistent with the patient's reported pain on active abduction between 60 and 120 degrees and a positive empty can test."
Related Words
rotator cuff (muscle group) infraspinatus (adjacent muscle) subscapularis (adjacent muscle) impingement syndrome (common pathology) supraspinous fossa (origin)

adductor
/əˈdʌk.tə/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
骨科 Musculoskeletal Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
A muscle that moves a limb or body part toward the midline of the body; in the lower limb, the hip adductor group (comprising adductor longus, brevis, and magnus, along with gracilis and pectineus) controls hip adduction and is commonly strained during sporting activities.
繁體中文
內收肌
Common Collocations
adductor strain hip adductors adductor longus adductor magnus adductor squeeze test
OET Clinical Example
"The patient sustained an acute left adductor strain during football training, presenting with medial thigh pain, reduced hip adduction strength graded 3/5 on the left, and a positive squeeze test at 0 degrees of hip flexion."
Related Words
abductor (antonym — moves away from midline) adduction (movement) groin strain (lay term) adductor longus / brevis / magnus (specific muscles) gracilis (synergist)

✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 98)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Mr. David Nguyen, a 72-year-old male with a ten-year history of idiopathic Parkinson's disease (Hoehn & Yahr Stage III), was referred to the outpatient physiotherapy department by his neurologist for assessment and management of progressive gait deterioration and falls.

On assessment, Mr. Nguyen demonstrated marked festination during forward ambulation, characterised by short, rapid shuffling steps with a progressive forward lean and an inability to self-arrest. He reported three falls in the preceding month, all occurring during turning or when navigating doorways. Freezing episodes were also noted during initiation of gait.

His carer reported significant lability since his recent medication adjustment, with episodes of unprovoked tearfulness that had increased in frequency over the past fortnight. This has been communicated to the treating neurologist for review.

A programme of cueing strategies, rhythmic auditory stimulation, and biofeedback-assisted gait retraining has been initiated, with the aim of reducing festination frequency and improving step length. Goals include safe indoor ambulation with minimal supervision and carer education regarding fall prevention strategies in the home environment.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When documenting neurological gait disorders in a referral letter, specify the nature of the abnormality (e.g. "festination with forward lean"), the functional consequences (e.g. "three falls in the preceding month"), and any precipitating contexts (e.g. "during turning or navigating doorways"). Avoid vague descriptors such as "unsteady" without qualification.
