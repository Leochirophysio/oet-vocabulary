empyema
/ˌem.paɪˈiː.mə/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
心肺 Cardiopulmonary Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
A collection of pus within a body cavity, most commonly the pleural space (between the lung and chest wall); arises as a complication of pneumonia, chest trauma, or surgery.
繁體中文
膿胸（胸腔積膿）
Common Collocations
pleural empyema empyema thoracis parapneumonic empyema drainage of empyema subdural empyema
OET Clinical Example
"Following the confirmed diagnosis of right-sided empyema, the patient underwent intercostal drain insertion; physiotherapy was requested to facilitate airway clearance and prevent further respiratory deterioration."
Related Words
pleural effusion (fluid — not pus) haemothorax (blood in pleural space) pyothorax (synonym) thoracocentesis (drainage procedure) suppurative (adj — pus-forming)

endocarditis
/ˌen.dəʊ.kɑːˈdaɪ.tɪs/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
心肺 Cardiopulmonary Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Inflammation of the endocardium — the inner lining of the heart chambers and valves — most commonly caused by bacterial infection; presents with fever, cardiac murmur, and embolic phenomena.
繁體中文
心內膜炎
Common Collocations
infective endocarditis bacterial endocarditis subacute endocarditis endocarditis prophylaxis rheumatic endocarditis
OET Clinical Example
"Mr. Patel has been admitted with a diagnosis of infective endocarditis affecting the mitral valve; physiotherapy is requested to maintain functional capacity and prevent deconditioning during his anticipated prolonged inpatient stay."
Related Words
endocardial (adj) myocarditis (heart muscle inflammation) pericarditis (outer lining inflammation) valvular disease bacteraemia (bacteria in bloodstream)
Word 3 of 5
fremitus
/ˈfrem.ɪ.tʌs/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
心肺 Cardiopulmonary Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
A palpable vibration or trembling felt on the chest wall during breathing or vocalisation; used in respiratory assessment — increased over consolidation, decreased or absent over pleural effusion or pneumothorax.
繁體中文
語音震顫（觸覺振顫）
Common Collocations
tactile fremitus vocal fremitus increased fremitus decreased fremitus absent fremitus
OET Clinical Example
"On chest assessment, tactile fremitus was markedly decreased over the right lower lobe, consistent with a large pleural effusion subsequently confirmed on chest radiograph."
Related Words
vocal resonance (auscultatory equivalent) bronchophony (increased vocal resonance) egophony (bleat-like resonance) percussion (chest assessment method) auscultation (listening with stethoscope)
Word 4 of 5
purulent
/ˈpjʊər.ʊ.lənt/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Signs & Symptoms 心肺 Cardiopulmonary
Medical Definition
Containing, consisting of, or producing pus; used to describe secretions, wound exudate, or discharge that indicates active infection or suppuration.
繁體中文
化膿的、含膿的
Common Collocations
purulent sputum purulent discharge purulent exudate purulent wound mucopurulent secretions
OET Clinical Example
"The patient was producing copious purulent sputum with an offensive odour, and twice-daily airway clearance physiotherapy using the active cycle of breathing technique was commenced to assist expectoration."
Related Words
pus (noun — the fluid itself) suppurative (adj — forming pus) purulence (noun) mucopurulent (mixed mucus and pus) serous (antonym — clear watery fluid)
Word 5 of 5
myocarditis
/ˌmaɪ.əʊ.kɑːˈdaɪ.tɪs/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
心肺 Cardiopulmonary Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Inflammation of the myocardium (the muscular wall of the heart), most commonly caused by viral infection; may present with chest pain, fatigue, palpitations, and in severe cases, acute heart failure or arrhythmia.
繁體中文
心肌炎
Common Collocations
viral myocarditis acute myocarditis fulminant myocarditis myocarditis-induced cardiomyopathy suspected myocarditis
OET Clinical Example
"In view of his recent diagnosis of viral myocarditis, exercise intensity has been restricted; cardiac rehabilitation is to commence only once written cardiological clearance has been obtained."
Related Words
myocardial (adj — relating to the heart muscle) cardiomyopathy (disease of the heart muscle) pericarditis (outer lining inflammation) endocarditis (inner lining inflammation) troponin (cardiac damage biomarker)
✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 85)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Mr. David Hoang, a 54-year-old male with a history of recent viral illness, was admitted under the cardiology team with a confirmed diagnosis of acute myocarditis. He presented with progressive exertional fatigue, intermittent chest discomfort, and marked deconditioning over the preceding three weeks.

On chest physiotherapy assessment, the patient was noted to be producing purulent sputum consistent with a concurrent lower respiratory tract infection. Chest examination revealed decreased tactile fremitus at the right base, and subsequent imaging confirmed a moderate parapneumonic pleural effusion.

The effusion progressed to a right-sided empyema requiring intercostal drain insertion by the respiratory team. Echocardiography additionally identified mild endocarditis of the tricuspid valve, for which the infectious diseases team commenced intravenous antibiotic therapy.

Physiotherapy goals include twice-daily airway clearance, progressive mobilisation within cardiac precautions, and patient education regarding activity pacing and symptom monitoring prior to planned cardiac rehabilitation referral on discharge.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When documenting respiratory findings, specify the location (e.g. "right base"), the clinical sign (e.g. "decreased fremitus"), and the confirmatory investigation (e.g. "confirmed on imaging"). Precise descriptors such as "purulent" convey clinical severity far more effectively than vague terms like "bad" or "infected."
