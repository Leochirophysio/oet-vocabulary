Word 1 of 5
trochanteric
/ˌtrɒk.ənˈter.ɪk/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
骨科 Musculoskeletal Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Relating to the trochanter, particularly the greater trochanter — the bony prominence on the lateral upper femur. Most commonly encountered clinically in "trochanteric bursitis" or "greater trochanteric pain syndrome," characterised by lateral hip pain and localised tenderness.
繁體中文
轉子的；大轉子（相關）的
Common Collocations
trochanteric bursitis trochanteric pain syndrome greater trochanteric trochanteric tenderness trochanteric region
OET Clinical Example
"The patient presented with a three-month history of right-sided trochanteric pain syndrome, reporting point tenderness over the greater trochanter and lateral hip pain aggravated by lying on the affected side and climbing stairs."
Related Words
greater trochanter (noun — bony landmark) bursitis (inflammation of bursa) iliotibial band (adjacent structure) gluteus medius (commonly involved muscle) lateral hip pain (lay description)
Word 2 of 5
arthrodesis
/ˌɑː.θrəʊˈdiː.sɪs/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
骨科 Musculoskeletal Procedures & Interventions
Medical Definition
A surgical procedure in which the cartilage surfaces of a joint are removed and the bones are fused together with internal fixation, permanently eliminating joint movement to relieve intractable pain; also called joint fusion.
繁體中文
關節固定術；骨融合術
Common Collocations
ankle arthrodesis subtalar arthrodesis wrist arthrodesis spinal arthrodesis triple arthrodesis
OET Clinical Example
"Mr. Patel was referred for post-operative physiotherapy following right ankle arthrodesis, with the surgical team requesting a structured weight-bearing programme and gait rehabilitation commencing at six weeks post-procedure."
Related Words
joint fusion (lay term) arthroplasty (antonym — joint replacement) internal fixation (surgical method) non-union (complication) arthrodesed (adj — past tense)
Word 3 of 5
discectomy
/dɪˈsek.tə.mi/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Procedures & Interventions 骨科 Musculoskeletal
Medical Definition
Surgical removal of all or part of an intervertebral disc, typically performed to decompress a nerve root or the spinal cord when disc herniation causes persistent radiculopathy or neurological deficit unresponsive to conservative management.
繁體中文
椎間盤切除術
Common Collocations
lumbar discectomy microdiscectomy cervical discectomy post-discectomy rehabilitation discectomy at L4/L5
OET Clinical Example
"The patient underwent lumbar microdiscectomy at L4/L5 eight weeks ago following failure of conservative management and has been referred for a graded rehabilitation programme targeting core stabilisation and a supervised return to occupational duties."
Related Words
herniation (indication for surgery) laminectomy (related decompression surgery) radiculopathy (nerve root compression) discogenic (adj — arising from disc) foraminotomy (widening the nerve exit)
Word 4 of 5
metacarpal
/ˌmet.əˈkɑː.pəl/ noun / adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
骨科 Musculoskeletal
Medical Definition
Any of the five cylindrical bones of the hand situated between the wrist (carpus) and the fingers (phalanges); they form the structural framework of the palm. Numbered one to five from the thumb side (radial) to the little finger side (ulnar).
繁體中文
掌骨（的）
Common Collocations
metacarpal fracture fifth metacarpal first metacarpal metacarpal head metacarpophalangeal joint
OET Clinical Example
"Radiograph confirmed an undisplaced fracture of the fifth metacarpal neck; the patient was referred for hand therapy following a period of buddy splinting to address oedema management, range-of-motion restoration, and grip strengthening."
Related Words
phalanges (distal — finger bones) carpals (proximal — wrist bones) metacarpophalangeal / MCP joint Boxer's fracture (informal — 5th metacarpal neck) metatarsal (equivalent foot bone)
Word 5 of 5
neuromobilisation
/ˌnjʊə.rəʊˌməʊ.bɪ.laɪˈzeɪ.ʃən/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Procedures & Interventions 骨科 Musculoskeletal
Medical Definition
A physiotherapy technique that applies graduated movement to neural tissue — such as the sciatic or median nerve — along its anatomical course, aiming to restore normal neurodynamic mobility, reduce adverse neural tension, and alleviate symptoms arising from restricted nerve movement.
繁體中文
神經鬆動術；神經組織動態鬆解
Common Collocations
lumbar neuromobilisation upper limb neuromobilisation progressive neuromobilisation sciatic neuromobilisation neural tissue mobilisation
OET Clinical Example
"Progressive lumbar neuromobilisation was incorporated into the treatment programme to address the patient's reported posterior thigh paraesthesia and a positive straight leg raise reproduction at 40 degrees bilaterally."
Related Words
neurodynamics (underlying concept) adverse neural tension (condition treated) straight leg raise / SLR (neural assessment) nerve gliding (lay term) neural flossing (technique variant)
✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 79)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Mr. James Toru, a 47-year-old male warehouse worker, was referred to your outpatient physiotherapy department following two separate orthopaedic procedures over the preceding four months.

Three months ago, Mr. Toru underwent lumbar microdiscectomy at L4/L5 for radiculopathy unresponsive to eight weeks of conservative management. At six weeks post-surgery, he additionally sustained an undisplaced fracture of the fifth metacarpal neck of the right (dominant) hand following a workplace incident, which was managed conservatively with buddy splinting for four weeks.

On initial physiotherapy assessment, Mr. Toru reported residual right-sided trochanteric discomfort, likely secondary to altered gait mechanics during his post-operative recovery period, as well as posterior thigh paraesthesia and a positive straight leg raise at 45 degrees bilaterally, indicating ongoing neural sensitisation.

The proposed management plan includes progressive neuromobilisation of the lumbar neural tissues, graded loading for the right hand commencing with intrinsic muscle strengthening and composite flexion exercises, and lateral hip rehabilitation targeting the trochanteric region with progressive loading of the gluteal musculature. The patient has been advised regarding activity modification and is keen to return to light occupational duties within eight weeks.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When a patient has multiple concurrent conditions, organise your referral letter clearly — use separate paragraphs for each problem, state the onset and management to date, and specify the rehabilitation goals for each. Avoid combining unrelated conditions in a single sentence.
