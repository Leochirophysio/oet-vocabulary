Word 1 of 5
prosopagnosia
/ˌprɒs.ə.pæɡˈnəʊ.zi.ə/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
神經 Neurological Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
A neurological condition in which a person is unable to recognise familiar faces despite intact visual acuity and general object recognition; may be congenital (developmental) or acquired following brain injury, typically involving the right fusiform gyrus.
繁體中文
臉孔失認症；臉盲症
Common Collocations
developmental prosopagnosia acquired prosopagnosia prosopagnosia following stroke prosopagnosia after TBI compensatory strategies for prosopagnosia
OET Clinical Example
"Mr Chen has been referred to the neuropsychology team for formal assessment of prosopagnosia identified following his right temporal lobe infarction; he is currently unable to recognise family members' faces and relies on voice and contextual cues for identification."
Related Words
agnosia (general recognition failure) visual agnosia topographical agnosia face blindness (lay term) fusiform face area (anatomy)
Word 2 of 5
echolalia
/ˌek.əʊˈleɪ.li.ə/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
神經 Neurological Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
The automatic and involuntary repetition of words or phrases just spoken by another person; commonly associated with autism spectrum disorder, Tourette syndrome, transcortical aphasias, and certain dementias. May be immediate or delayed.
繁體中文
仿語症；回聲語言
Common Collocations
immediate echolalia delayed echolalia functional echolalia echolalia in ASD mitigated echolalia
OET Clinical Example
"During the speech pathology assessment, Tommy demonstrated prominent immediate echolalia, consistently repeating the final phrase of each question posed rather than generating independent verbal responses; this was noted to interfere significantly with functional communication."
Related Words
palilalia (repetition of own words) echopraxia (repetition of actions) echophrasia (synonym) perseveration transcortical aphasia
Word 3 of 5
acalculia
/ˌeɪ.kæl.kjʊˈliː.ə/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
神經 Neurological Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
An acquired neurological impairment of the ability to perform arithmetic calculations, typically resulting from damage to the parietal lobe, particularly the left angular gyrus; distinct from dyscalculia, which is a developmental learning difficulty.
繁體中文
計算不能症；失算症
Common Collocations
primary acalculia secondary acalculia acalculia following stroke acalculia with parietal lobe lesion assessment of acalculia
OET Clinical Example
"Neuropsychological testing revealed acalculia alongside constructional apraxia, consistent with left parietal lobe involvement; specialist occupational therapy input has been requested to address the functional impact on the patient's return to work as an accountant."
Related Words
dyscalculia (developmental, partial) agraphia (inability to write) alexia (inability to read) finger agnosia Gerstmann syndrome (cluster)
Word 4 of 5
psychosocial
/ˌsaɪ.kəʊˈsəʊ.ʃəl/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Patient Communication
Medical Definition
Relating to the interplay between psychological and social factors in relation to health, illness, and recovery; encompasses emotional, cognitive, interpersonal, and environmental dimensions of a patient's experience and wellbeing.
繁體中文
心理社會的；心理社交的
Common Collocations
psychosocial factors psychosocial assessment psychosocial support psychosocial barriers to recovery psychosocial well-being
OET Clinical Example
"A comprehensive psychosocial assessment was conducted prior to discharge, identifying fear of re-injury and limited social support as significant barriers to the patient's engagement with the prescribed home exercise programme."
Related Words
biopsychosocial (broader model) psychological sociocultural yellow flags (psychosocial risk factors) psychosocial rehabilitation
Word 5 of 5
contraindication
/ˌkɒn.trə.ˌɪn.dɪˈkeɪ.ʃən/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Procedures & Interventions Patient Communication
Medical Definition
A specific condition, factor, or circumstance that makes a particular treatment, procedure, or medication inadvisable because it may cause harm; classified as absolute (treatment must not be used) or relative (use with caution).
繁體中文
禁忌症；反適應症
Common Collocations
absolute contraindication relative contraindication contraindication to manual therapy identify contraindications no contraindications noted
OET Clinical Example
"Manual spinal traction was identified as a contraindication in this patient given the confirmed diagnosis of severe osteoporosis on DEXA scan and a recent L2 vertebral compression fracture; alternative decompression strategies have been implemented accordingly."
Related Words
indication (antonym — reason to use) precaution (relative caution) contraindicated (adjective) contraindicate (verb) red flags (clinical warning signs)
✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 87)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Mr David Tran, a 54-year-old male, was admitted following a right middle cerebral artery stroke three weeks ago. He has been referred to the physiotherapy and allied health team for ongoing inpatient rehabilitation.

Neuropsychological assessment has identified several cognitive sequelae. Mr Tran demonstrates prosopagnosia, being unable to reliably identify his wife or adult children by sight alone, and relies on vocal cues for recognition. Additionally, acalculia has been confirmed, which is of functional significance given his prior employment as a small business owner.

A thorough psychosocial assessment conducted by the social work team identified significant anxiety related to his cognitive deficits and financial concerns regarding his business, both of which represent barriers to rehabilitation engagement. The team has recommended counselling and peer support referral.

With respect to physiotherapy management, aggressive cervical manipulation is a contraindication given the acute post-stroke period and anticoagulation therapy currently in progress. Gentle mobilisation and progressive gait retraining have been commenced within these precautions.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When documenting cognitive deficits in a referral, specify each impairment by name (e.g. "prosopagnosia", "acalculia") and its functional impact (e.g. "unable to identify family members", "impairs return to work"). Always state contraindications and the rationale, then describe what alternative management has been implemented.
