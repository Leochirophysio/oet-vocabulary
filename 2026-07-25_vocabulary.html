diastolic
/ˌdaɪ.əˈstɒl.ɪk/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
心肺 Cardiopulmonary
Medical Definition
Relating to diastole — the phase of the cardiac cycle during which the heart muscle relaxes and the ventricles fill with blood; the diastolic blood pressure reading (the lower number) reflects arterial pressure during this resting phase.
繁體中文
舒張期的；舒張壓（名詞用法）
Common Collocations
diastolic blood pressure diastolic dysfunction diastolic heart failure diastolic filling elevated diastolic pressure
OET Clinical Example
"On admission, the patient's blood pressure was recorded at 158/96 mmHg; the persistently elevated diastolic reading of 96 mmHg was consistent with longstanding hypertensive disease and prompted medication review."
Related Words
systolic (antonym — contraction phase) diastole (noun) cardiac cycle hypertension pulse pressure (systolic minus diastolic)
Word 2 of 5
haematuria
/ˌhiː.məˈtjʊə.ri.ə/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
The presence of blood in the urine; may be visible to the naked eye (gross or macroscopic haematuria, producing red or brown discolouration) or detectable only by urine dipstick or microscopy (microscopic haematuria).
繁體中文
血尿
Common Collocations
gross haematuria microscopic haematuria frank haematuria painless haematuria macroscopic haematuria
OET Clinical Example
"The patient reported a three-day history of frank haematuria associated with suprapubic discomfort; urinalysis confirmed the presence of blood and leucocytes, and the patient was referred urgently to urology for cystoscopy."
Related Words
hematuria (American spelling) haematuric (adj) proteinuria (protein in urine) pyuria (pus in urine) urinalysis (diagnostic test)
Word 3 of 5
oliguria
/ˌɒl.ɪˈɡjʊə.ri.ə/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Abnormally reduced urine output, defined in adults as less than 0.5 mL/kg/hour (or less than approximately 400 mL per day); an important clinical indicator of dehydration, acute kidney injury, or haemodynamic compromise.
繁體中文
少尿
Common Collocations
acute oliguria persistent oliguria post-operative oliguria oliguria secondary to oliguria and fluid overload
OET Clinical Example
"The surgical team was alerted to the patient's persistent oliguria following the procedure, with hourly urine output falling below 20 mL for three consecutive hours, suggesting impending acute kidney injury."
Related Words
anuria (no urine output) polyuria (excess urine output) oliguric (adj) diuresis (increased urine output) acute kidney injury / AKI
Word 4 of 5
crepitations
/ˌkrep.ɪˈteɪ.ʃənz/ noun (plural) 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
心肺 Cardiopulmonary Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Abnormal crackling or rattling breath sounds heard on auscultation of the lungs, produced by the sudden opening of collapsed small airways or by fluid in the alveoli; also called rales or crackles. Distinct from crepitus, which refers to a palpable or audible sound in joints or soft tissue.
繁體中文
肺部爆裂音；囉音（聽診所聞）
Common Collocations
fine crepitations coarse crepitations bibasal crepitations bilateral crepitations crepitations on auscultation
OET Clinical Example
"Chest auscultation performed by the physiotherapist revealed fine bibasal crepitations bilaterally, consistent with pulmonary oedema secondary to acute cardiac decompensation, prompting immediate escalation to the medical team."
Related Words
rales (synonym — predominantly American) crackles (common clinical synonym) crepitus (joint sound — different) rhonchi (low-pitched wheeze) auscultation (method of detection)
Word 5 of 5
valvular
/ˈvæl.vjʊ.lər/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
心肺 Cardiopulmonary
Medical Definition
Relating to or affecting the valves of the heart; used to describe conditions, abnormalities, or procedures involving the cardiac valves (mitral, aortic, tricuspid, or pulmonary) that regulate the flow of blood between the heart's chambers and great vessels.
繁體中文
瓣膜的；心瓣膜相關的
Common Collocations
valvular heart disease valvular regurgitation valvular stenosis valvular dysfunction valvular lesion
OET Clinical Example
"The echocardiogram confirmed moderate mitral valvular regurgitation with preserved left ventricular function; the patient was referred to cardiology for ongoing management prior to commencing the outpatient cardiac rehabilitation programme."
Related Words
valve (noun) regurgitation (backflow through valve) stenosis (valve narrowing) valvuloplasty (balloon procedure) mitral / aortic / tricuspid / pulmonary (valve types)
✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 78)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Mr Tran Van Minh, a 72-year-old male with a background of ischaemic heart disease, longstanding valvular heart disease (known moderate mitral regurgitation), and poorly controlled hypertension, was admitted to the medical ward following a two-day history of worsening exertional breathlessness and ankle oedema.

On admission, his blood pressure was 154/98 mmHg. The persistently elevated diastolic reading of 98 mmHg, in the context of his known cardiac history, prompted urgent cardiology review. Chest auscultation performed by the physiotherapy team revealed fine bibasal crepitations bilaterally, consistent with pulmonary oedema secondary to decompensated cardiac failure.

Urinary output was closely monitored following diuretic therapy initiation. The nursing team documented oliguria over the first six hours of admission, with hourly output averaging 18 mL, raising concern for haemodynamic compromise and early acute kidney injury. In addition, urinalysis on admission revealed haematuria, and nephrology was contacted for concurrent assessment and consideration of further urological investigation.

Physiotherapy input was focused on daily chest clearance techniques, positioning for ventilatory optimisation, and graded mobility as haemodynamic status permitted.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When reporting cardiopulmonary findings in a referral letter, always specify laterality (e.g. "bibasal crepitations bilaterally"), the specific reading for vital signs (e.g. "diastolic reading of 98 mmHg"), and clinical context (e.g. "consistent with pulmonary oedema secondary to decompensated cardiac failure"). Precise language demonstrates clinical competence to the OET marker.
