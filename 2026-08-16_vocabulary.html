syndesmosis
/ˌsɪn.dezˈməʊ.sɪs/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
骨科 Musculoskeletal
Medical Definition
A type of slightly movable fibrous joint in which two bones are held together by interosseous ligamentous tissue; the distal tibiofibular syndesmosis is clinically significant in high ankle sprains, where disruption results in widening of the ankle mortise and instability.
繁體中文
韌帶聯合
Common Collocations
distal tibiofibular syndesmosis syndesmosis injury syndesmosis sprain syndesmosis instability syndesmosis repair
OET Clinical Example
"Stress radiographs confirmed disruption of the distal tibiofibular syndesmosis with 3 mm diastasis; the patient was referred for operative fixation and subsequent physiotherapy rehabilitation."
Related Words
syndesmotic (adj) amphiarthrosis (slightly movable joint) diastasis (abnormal separation) ankle mortise high ankle sprain

autograft
/ˈɔː.tə.ɡrɑːft/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
骨科 Musculoskeletal Procedures & Interventions
Medical Definition
A surgical graft consisting of tissue transplanted from one anatomical site to another within the same individual's body; commonly used in ligament reconstruction (e.g. hamstring or patellar tendon autograft for ACL repair) and bone grafting procedures.
繁體中文
自體移植（自體組織移植）
Common Collocations
hamstring autograft patellar tendon autograft bone-tendon-bone autograft autograft harvest autograft reconstruction
OET Clinical Example
"Mr Nguyen underwent ACL reconstruction using a quadruple-strand hamstring autograft; post-operative physiotherapy should include monitoring of donor site morbidity at the pes anserinus alongside the standard ligament rehabilitation protocol."
Related Words
allograft (from a donor) xenograft (from an animal) autologous (adj) graft harvest donor site morbidity

intervertebral
/ˌɪn.tə.ˈvɜː.tɪ.brəl/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
骨科 Musculoskeletal
Medical Definition
Situated or occurring between adjacent vertebral bodies; most commonly used to describe the intervertebral disc—the fibrocartilaginous structure that acts as a shock absorber—and the intervertebral foramen, through which spinal nerve roots exit the vertebral canal.
繁體中文
椎間的
Common Collocations

OET Clinical Example
"MRI of the lumbar spine demonstrated L4/5 intervertebral disc herniation with moderate right foraminal compromise, consistent with the patient's reported right L5 dermatomal distribution of pain and paraesthesia."
Related Words
discogenic (adj — disc origin) nucleus pulposus (inner disc) annulus fibrosus (outer disc ring) radiculopathy (nerve root compression) foraminal stenosis
Word 4 of 5
plica
/ˈplaɪ.kə/ noun (plural: plicae) 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
骨科 Musculoskeletal Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
A fold of synovial membrane within a joint, most commonly encountered in the knee as a remnant of the embryological synovial septum; when a plica becomes thickened and fibrotic through overuse or trauma, it may impinge on articular surfaces, causing anterior or medial knee pain known as plica syndrome.
繁體中文
滑膜皺襞
Common Collocations
medial plica synovial plica plica syndrome symptomatic plica plica resection
OET Clinical Example
"Arthroscopic assessment identified a thickened medial plica impinging upon the medial femoral condyle during knee flexion; plica resection was performed and the patient has been referred for post-operative physiotherapy to restore full range of motion."
Related Words
plicae (plural) synovial fold (synonym) plica syndrome (condition) synovectomy (surgical removal) arthroscopy (diagnostic/treatment)
Word 5 of 5
extracorporeal
/ˌek.strə.kɔːˈpɔː.ri.əl/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Procedures & Interventions
Medical Definition
Occurring or applied outside the living body; in physiotherapy, most commonly refers to extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT), a non-invasive treatment using high-energy acoustic waves delivered through the skin to stimulate tissue healing and reduce pain in chronic musculoskeletal conditions.
繁體中文
體外的（體外衝擊波治療）
Common Collocations
extracorporeal shockwave therapy radial extracorporeal shockwave focused extracorporeal shockwave extracorporeal treatment extracorporeal application
OET Clinical Example
"Given the patient's 12-month history of recalcitrant insertional Achilles tendinopathy unresponsive to conservative management, a referral for extracorporeal shockwave therapy has been arranged as the next line of treatment prior to surgical consideration."
Related Words
ESWT (abbreviation) shockwave therapy (shortened form) non-invasive (characteristic) tendinopathy (common indication) phonophoresis (related electrotherapy)
✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 93)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Mr Santos, a 32-year-old recreational rugby player, is referred to your physiotherapy department following ACL reconstruction performed using a patellar tendon autograft. Pre-operative arthroscopic assessment also identified a symptomatic medial plica in the same knee, which was resected during the same operative session.

His rehabilitation has progressed appropriately through the initial phase; however, he reports persistent anterior knee pain localised to the autograft donor site, consistent with reactive patellar tendinopathy. Conservative management over the preceding eight weeks—including eccentric loading and manual therapy—has yielded limited improvement.

As the tendinopathic donor site is impeding progression to the strength and conditioning phase of his ACL protocol, a course of extracorporeal shockwave therapy has been arranged. It is anticipated that this will reduce tendon irritability and allow full engagement with the rehabilitation programme.

Please note an incidental finding of mild L5/S1 intervertebral disc degeneration on pre-operative MRI; this is currently asymptomatic and does not require active physiotherapy intervention at this time.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When a patient has multiple concurrent pathologies, address each one explicitly and clearly state which requires active management and which is being monitored ("currently asymptomatic — does not require active intervention"). This demonstrates clinical reasoning and prevents the receiving clinician from missing secondary diagnoses.
