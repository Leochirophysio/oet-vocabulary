✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 86)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Mr. James Taukiri, a 34-year-old male construction worker, was referred to the physiotherapy department following surgical management of a comminuted fracture of the left distal radius sustained in a workplace fall from height three months ago.

The patient underwent open reduction and internal fixation, followed by a six-week period of immobilisation in a below-elbow plaster cast. He was subsequently referred for physiotherapy to address significant stiffness, reduced grip strength, and restricted forearm rotation.

On initial assessment, MRI findings were also noted to demonstrate an associated tear of the triangular fibrocartilage complex (TFCC), which was discussed with the patient in terms of its potential contribution to ongoing wrist pain and instability. Prior to this referral, an intra-articular corticosteroid injection had been administered to the radiocarpal joint to reduce synovial inflammation and improve tolerance of early active movement.

Current physiotherapy management includes graded range-of-motion exercises, grip strengthening, and phonophoresis with a diclofenac medium applied over the dorsal wrist to address residual soft-tissue inflammation. Goals are to restore functional wrist movement sufficient for a return to light duties within eight weeks.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When documenting procedures in a referral letter, name the specific intervention (e.g. "intra-articular corticosteroid injection"), the anatomical site (e.g. "radiocarpal joint"), and the clinical rationale (e.g. "to reduce synovial inflammation"). Precision signals clinical competence to the receiving clinician.
