Word 1 of 5
perinatal
/ˌper.ɪˈneɪ.t(ə)l/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
小兒 Paediatric Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Relating to the period immediately before and after birth; conventionally defined as from 22 weeks of gestation to seven days after delivery. Used to describe conditions, injuries, or events occurring during this critical developmental window.
繁體中文
圍產期的、圍生期的
Common Collocations
perinatal asphyxia perinatal hypoxia perinatal injury perinatal period perinatal outcome
OET Clinical Example
"The infant was referred for developmental physiotherapy assessment following a documented history of perinatal asphyxia, with Apgar scores of 3 at one minute and 6 at five minutes of life."
Related Words
antenatal / prenatal (before birth) postnatal (after birth) intrapartum (during labour) neonatal (first 28 days) perinatology (noun — the specialty)
Word 2 of 5
gestational
/dʒesˈteɪ.ʃ(ə)n.əl/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
小兒 Paediatric
Medical Definition
Relating to the period of gestation — the development of the fetus in the uterus from fertilisation to birth. In paediatric practice, "gestational age" expresses how far along a pregnancy is, and "corrected gestational age" is used to adjust developmental expectations in premature infants.
繁體中文
妊娠的、胎齡的
Common Collocations
gestational age corrected gestational age gestational diabetes gestational hypertension gestational week
OET Clinical Example
"The infant was born at 29 weeks gestational age and is currently being reviewed at a corrected age of four months; developmental milestones are being assessed accordingly."
Related Words
gestation (noun) corrected age / adjusted age prematurity (born before 37 weeks) term (40 weeks gestation) antenatal (adj — before birth)
Word 3 of 5
regurgitation
/rɪˌɡɜː.dʒɪˈteɪ.ʃ(ə)n/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
小兒 Paediatric Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
The passive backward flow of stomach contents into the oesophagus or mouth without the forceful muscular effort of vomiting. In infants, it is a common manifestation of gastro-oesophageal reflux. In cardiology, it refers to the backward flow of blood through a defective heart valve.
繁體中文
逆流、反流；（嬰兒）溢奶
Common Collocations
frequent regurgitation post-feed regurgitation acid regurgitation mitral regurgitation symptomatic regurgitation
OET Clinical Example
"The parents reported persistent post-feed regurgitation associated with marked irritability and back-arching, consistent with symptomatic gastro-oesophageal reflux disease requiring further medical review."
Related Words
regurgitate (verb) reflux (clinical / lay term) GORD (gastro-oesophageal reflux disease) vomiting (forceful — contrast) aspiration (complication)
Word 4 of 5
exertional
/ɪɡˈzɜː.ʃ(ə)n.əl/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Occurring during or brought on by physical effort or exercise; used to describe symptoms that appear specifically with activity rather than at rest. Commonly paired with dyspnoea, chest pain, or headache to describe effort-related complaints.
繁體中文
運動性的、勞力性的
Common Collocations
exertional dyspnoea exertional chest pain exertional angina exertional headache exertional intolerance
OET Clinical Example
"Mr. Tran reported exertional chest tightness occurring after climbing one flight of stairs, which resolved within five minutes of rest and was not accompanied by diaphoresis or radiation to the arm."
Related Words
exertion (noun) exercise-induced (synonym) on exertion (clinical phrase) effort-related postexertional (after exertion)
Word 5 of 5
prodromal
/prəʊˈdrəʊ.m(ə)l/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Relating to the early warning stage of a disease, occurring before the characteristic or full-blown symptoms appear. Prodromal symptoms serve as early indicators that a condition is developing or that a relapse is imminent.
繁體中文
前驅期的、先兆的
Common Collocations
prodromal symptoms prodromal phase prodromal period prodromal headache prodromal stage
OET Clinical Example
"The patient described a prodromal phase of generalised fatigue and diffuse myalgia persisting for 48 hours prior to the onset of frank joint swelling and erythema, consistent with the early inflammatory stage of this condition."
Related Words
prodrome / prodromes (noun) premonitory (synonym) aura (migraine-specific) precursor symptoms early warning signs
✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 88)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Baby Aroha, a three-month-old female born at 31 weeks gestational age (corrected age: six weeks), was referred to the paediatric physiotherapy team for assessment and management of feeding difficulties and positional concerns.

Her mother reported frequent post-feed regurgitation occurring after nearly every feed, associated with distress and extension of the neck and trunk. A diagnosis of gastro-oesophageal reflux disease had been confirmed by the neonatology team prior to discharge. Background history was notable for a perinatal course complicated by respiratory distress syndrome requiring 18 days of ventilatory support.

On assessment, Aroha demonstrated mild axial hypotonia and asymmetrical head positioning with a right-sided preference. The physiotherapy programme will focus on positioning strategies to reduce regurgitation events, tummy time facilitation, and parental education regarding developmental expectations at her corrected gestational age.

The parents were counselled regarding prodromal signs of feed intolerance — including persistent irritability, arching, and reduced wet nappies — and were advised to seek prompt medical review should these features escalate or if exertional distress during feeding was noted.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When writing paediatric referrals, always document corrected gestational age for premature infants rather than chronological age alone, and specify whether milestones are being assessed against corrected or actual age. Use precise clinical language: "post-feed regurgitation" rather than "spitting up."
