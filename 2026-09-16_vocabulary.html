vasovagal
/ˌveɪ.zəʊˈveɪ.ɡəl/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
心肺 Cardiopulmonary Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Relating to or caused by simultaneous stimulation of the vagus nerve producing vasodilation and bradycardia, leading to a sudden fall in blood pressure and transient loss of consciousness; characteristically triggered by emotional stress, pain, or prolonged standing.
繁體中文
血管迷走神經性（的）
Common Collocations
vasovagal syncope vasovagal episode vasovagal response vasovagal reflex vasovagal collapse
OET Clinical Example
"The patient experienced a vasovagal episode during the initial standing tolerance assessment, with sudden pallor, diaphoresis, and a transient loss of consciousness lasting approximately 10 seconds; the session was discontinued and a medical review was requested."
Related Words
syncope (resulting event) neurocardiogenic syncope (synonym) vagal (adjective root) bradycardia (associated sign) presyncope (near-faint)

capillary
/kəˈpɪl.ər.i/ noun / adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
心肺 Cardiopulmonary Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
The smallest blood vessels in the body, forming a network between arterioles and venules that enables exchange of oxygen, nutrients, and metabolic waste between blood and surrounding tissues; clinically assessed via capillary refill time (CRT) to evaluate peripheral perfusion.
繁體中文
微血管（毛細血管）
Common Collocations
capillary refill time capillary bed prolonged capillary refill capillary leak capillary blood glucose
OET Clinical Example
"Assessment of the right lower limb revealed a capillary refill time of 4 seconds, indicating compromised peripheral perfusion; the findings were documented and the treating physician notified for urgent review."
Related Words
arteriole (feeding vessel) venule (draining vessel) microcirculation (broader system) CRT (abbreviation) peripheral perfusion (clinical goal)

thrombosis
/θrɒmˈbəʊ.sɪs/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
心肺 Cardiopulmonary Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
The formation of a blood clot (thrombus) within a blood vessel, obstructing blood flow; occurs in veins (venous thrombosis, most commonly deep vein thrombosis) or arteries (arterial thrombosis); a major risk in immobile post-operative patients and a key physiotherapy red flag.
繁體中文
血栓症
Common Collocations
deep vein thrombosis venous thrombosis arterial thrombosis thrombosis risk factors thrombosis prophylaxis
OET Clinical Example
"In light of the patient's prolonged post-operative immobility and unilateral calf swelling with localised warmth, deep vein thrombosis was suspected; physiotherapy was withheld pending Doppler ultrasound confirmation and anticoagulation review."
Related Words
thrombus (the clot itself) embolism (displaced clot) thrombotic (adjective) DVT (abbreviation) anticoagulation (treatment)

polycythaemia
/ˌpɒl.i.saɪˈθiː.mi.ə/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
心肺 Cardiopulmonary Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
An abnormal increase in the number of red blood cells in the blood, resulting in elevated haematocrit and blood viscosity; secondary polycythaemia is a compensatory response to chronic hypoxaemia, commonly seen in patients with severe COPD or chronic cardiopulmonary conditions.
繁體中文
紅血球增多症
Common Collocations
secondary polycythaemia polycythaemia vera compensatory polycythaemia polycythaemia rubra vera polycythaemia management
OET Clinical Example
"The patient's recent blood results were notable for secondary polycythaemia with a haematocrit of 58%, attributed to longstanding hypoxaemia in the context of severe COPD; haematology review was arranged prior to commencement of a supervised pulmonary rehabilitation programme."
Related Words
erythrocytosis (synonym) polycythaemia vera (primary form) haematocrit (measurement) hyperviscosity (consequence) thrombosis risk (complication)
✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 99)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Mr. David Faleolo, a 61-year-old male with a background of severe COPD (FEV1 38% predicted) and secondary polycythaemia, was referred to the cardiopulmonary physiotherapy team for pulmonary rehabilitation following a recent exacerbation requiring hospitalisation.

On initial assessment, auscultation revealed bronchial breath sounds with reduced air entry bilaterally at the bases, and capillary refill time was measured at 3 seconds in both hands, consistent with his known chronic hypoxaemia. SpO2 at rest was 88% on room air, improving to 94% on 2 L/min supplemental oxygen.

During the stand-to-sit transfer component of the assessment, the patient reported light-headedness and near-collapse; this was consistent with a vasovagal response, likely precipitated by orthostatic stress and breath-holding. The session was suspended, the patient returned to the supine position, and observations were monitored until he returned to baseline.

Of note, the patient's right calf circumference was 3 cm greater than the left, with localised warmth and tenderness on palpation, raising clinical concern for deep vein thrombosis. Physiotherapy was withheld pending urgent Doppler ultrasound and haematology review given his elevated thrombotic risk secondary to polycythaemia.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When documenting a clinical incident (e.g. a vasovagal episode), always record the trigger, patient response, action taken, and outcome. For suspected DVT, document the asymmetry, clinical signs, and decision to withhold physiotherapy pending investigation — this demonstrates safe clinical reasoning in OET writing tasks.
