✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 96)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Mr. James O'Brien, a 36-year-old male competitive cyclist, was referred to the physiotherapy department following surgical fixation of a right acetabular fracture sustained in a road traffic accident three months ago. Postoperative imaging demonstrated satisfactory fracture reduction; however, MRI of the right hip revealed persistent periarticular hyperaemia, consistent with an ongoing inflammatory response at the surgical site.

Of note, the patient also presented with an established malunion of the left distal radius, sustained in a separate fall twelve months prior and managed conservatively. This has resulted in 20 degrees of dorsal angulation with restricted pronation to 40 degrees, limiting his ability to grip the handlebars and return to cycling.

Additionally, Mr. O'Brien reported bilateral acroparaesthesia of the hands, particularly nocturnal and upon waking, which commenced following his prolonged period of reduced activity. Nerve conduction studies are pending. It is also noted that prior to his accident, the patient suffered a hospital admission for exercise-induced rhabdomyolysis following an ultramarathon; serum creatine kinase had peaked at 38,000 U/L and resolved with aggressive hydration.

Goals of physiotherapy include progressive right hip rehabilitation, wrist range-of-motion exercises, and patient education regarding load management to prevent recurrence of muscle breakdown.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When documenting multiple concurrent conditions, use clear transitional phrases such as "Of note," "Additionally," and "It is also noted that" to guide the reader through complex clinical histories without ambiguity.
