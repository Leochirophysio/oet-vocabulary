sesamoid
/ˈses.ə.mɔɪd/ adjective / noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
骨科 Musculoskeletal
Medical Definition
A small, oval bone embedded within a tendon, typically located over a bony prominence at a joint. Sesamoid bones protect tendons from compressive forces and improve the mechanical advantage of the muscles they serve. The patella is the largest sesamoid bone in the body.
繁體中文
籽骨
Common Collocations
sesamoid bone sesamoid stress fracture bipartite sesamoid sesamoiditis tibial sesamoid
OET Clinical Example
"Plain radiograph confirmed a stress fracture of the tibial sesamoid of the right hallux, consistent with the patient's reported plantar pain on weight-bearing and push-off phase of gait."
Related Words
sesamoiditis (inflammation) bipartite sesamoid (congenital variant) patella (largest sesamoid) tendon (embedding structure) hallux (great toe)

radicular
/rəˈdɪk.jʊ.lər/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
骨科 Musculoskeletal Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Relating to or arising from a spinal nerve root; describes pain, paraesthesia, weakness, or other neurological signs that radiate along the distribution of a compressed or irritated nerve root.

神經根性的
Common Collocations

OET Clinical Example
"The patient presented with right-sided radicular pain radiating from the lumbar spine to the lateral aspect of the lower leg and dorsum of the foot, consistent with an L5 nerve root irritation secondary to disc herniation at L4/5."
Related Words
radiculopathy (noun — disease of nerve root) radiculitis (inflammation of nerve root) dermatomal (relating to dermatome) sciatica (L4–S1 radicular pain) referred pain (non-dermatomal)
Word 3 of 5
trophic
/ˈtrɒf.ɪk/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Relating to the nourishment and maintenance of tissues; used clinically to describe characteristic changes in skin, hair, and nails that result from impaired nerve supply or circulation, as seen in conditions such as Complex Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS) and peripheral neuropathy.
繁體中文
營養性的；神經營養的
Common Collocations
trophic changes trophic skin changes trophic ulcer trophic oedema dystrophic changes
OET Clinical Example
"On examination of the right hand, notable trophic changes were observed, including shiny, atrophic skin, brittle nails, and altered hair growth, alongside allodynia and vasomotor instability, raising clinical suspicion of Complex Regional Pain Syndrome Type I."
Related Words
dystrophic (impaired nourishment) atrophic (wasting) CRPS (Complex Regional Pain Syndrome) autonomic (nervous system) vasomotor (relating to blood vessel tone)
Word 4 of 5
derangement
/dɪˈreɪndʒ.mənt/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
骨科 Musculoskeletal Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
A disruption or disorganisation of normal anatomical structure or joint mechanics; most commonly applied in orthopaedics as "internal derangement," referring to disorders of intra-articular structures such as the menisci, ligaments, or articular cartilage, which cause pain, instability, locking, or restricted movement.
繁體中文
內部結構紊亂；關節損傷
Common Collocations
internal derangement internal derangement of the knee (IDK) joint derangement meniscal derangement patellofemoral derangement
OET Clinical Example
"MRI of the right knee confirmed internal derangement with a posterior horn medial meniscus tear and associated joint effusion; the patient was referred to physiotherapy for conservative management prior to any surgical consideration."
Related Words
disruption (synonym) instability (consequence) effusion (associated finding) meniscal tear (common cause) locking (symptom of loose body)

✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 103)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Mr. James Tran, a 42-year-old male construction worker, was referred to the physiotherapy outpatient department following a work-related injury to his right lower limb sustained three weeks prior.

The patient presented with right-sided lumbar spine pain with radicular symptoms extending to the lateral calf and dorsum of the foot in a dermatomal distribution consistent with L5 nerve root involvement. MRI confirmed disc herniation at L4/5 with moderate foraminal stenosis. Additionally, he reported ongoing right forefoot pain localised to the first metatarsophalangeal joint, with imaging demonstrating a stress fracture of the fibular sesamoid.

Of note, examination of the right lower limb revealed early trophic skin changes over the dorsum of the foot, including altered skin texture and reduced hair growth, alongside vasomotor instability. Neurovascular status of the right foot was assessed and documented as intact, with capillary refill time of 2 seconds, sensation within normal limits to light touch in all dermatomes, and preserved active toe movement.

Goals of physiotherapy include pain management, nerve root mobilisation, graduated weight-bearing rehabilitation for the sesamoid fracture, and monitoring of trophic changes for signs of Complex Regional Pain Syndrome. A gradual return-to-work programme has been discussed with the patient.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When documenting neurological involvement, specify both the distribution (e.g. "L5 dermatomal pattern") and the nature of symptoms (e.g. "radicular pain" vs "referred pain"). Document neurovascular checks with objective findings — capillary refill time, sensation, and movement — rather than simply stating "circulation intact."
