jugular
/ˈdʒʌɡ.jʊ.lə/ adjective / noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
心肺 Cardiopulmonary Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Relating to or located in the region of the neck, particularly the jugular veins; in clinical assessment, jugular venous pressure (JVP) is measured to evaluate right heart function and central venous pressure.
繁體中文
頸靜脈的；頸靜脈
Common Collocations
jugular venous pressure (JVP) raised jugular venous pressure jugular venous distension internal jugular vein jugular vein engorgement
OET Clinical Example
"On examination, the patient demonstrated markedly raised jugular venous pressure at 6 cm above the sternal angle, consistent with fluid overload secondary to decompensated right heart failure."
Related Words
JVP (abbreviation — jugular venous pressure) central venous pressure (CVP) hepatojugular reflux right heart failure venous distension

haemoglobin
/ˌhiː.məˈɡləʊ.bɪn/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
心肺 Cardiopulmonary Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
The iron-containing protein found in red blood cells that binds oxygen in the lungs and transports it to body tissues; also carries carbon dioxide back to the lungs for exhalation. Low haemoglobin is the defining feature of anaemia.
繁體中文
血紅蛋白；血色素
Common Collocations
haemoglobin level low haemoglobin haemoglobin concentration haemoglobin saturation glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c)
OET Clinical Example
"The patient's haemoglobin was reported at 82 g/L on recent bloods, indicating moderate anaemia, which was identified as a significant contributing factor to her reduced exercise tolerance and persistent fatigue during the physiotherapy programme."
Related Words
haemoglobin A / HbA (normal adult form) anaemia (low haemoglobin) polycythaemia (elevated haemoglobin) SpO2 (peripheral oxygen saturation) haematocrit (proportion of red cells)

hypoxic
/haɪˈpɒk.sɪk/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
心肺 Cardiopulmonary Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Describing a state in which the body or a region of tissue is deprived of an adequate supply of oxygen; relating to or caused by hypoxia. Often used to characterise a patient's clinical status or a pathological condition involving insufficient oxygen delivery.
繁體中文
缺氧的；低氧的
Common Collocations
hypoxic episode hypoxic drive hypoxic brain injury acutely hypoxic hypoxic respiratory failure
OET Clinical Example
"The patient became acutely hypoxic during the mobilisation session, with SpO2 decreasing to 84% on room air, necessitating immediate cessation of the activity and commencement of supplemental oxygen via nasal cannulae."
Related Words
hypoxia (noun — low tissue O2) hypoxaemia (low blood O2) anoxic (absent O2) cyanosis (sign of low O2) SpO2 / oxygen saturation

pericardial
/ˌper.ɪˈkɑː.di.əl/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
心肺 Cardiopulmonary Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Relating to the pericardium — the fibrous, double-layered sac that encloses the heart and the roots of the great vessels; used to describe conditions, findings, or structures associated with the pericardial space or membrane.
繁體中文
心包的；心包膜的
Common Collocations
pericardial effusion pericardial friction rub pericardial tamponade pericardial disease pericardial drainage
OET Clinical Example
"Echocardiography confirmed a moderate pericardial effusion with evidence of early haemodynamic compromise; the patient was referred urgently to cardiology for consideration of pericardiocentesis prior to commencement of cardiac rehabilitation."
Related Words
pericardium (noun — the sac itself) pericarditis (inflammation) pericardiocentesis (drainage procedure) tamponade (cardiac compression from effusion) epicardial (relating to the epicardium)
✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 92)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Mr. David Tran, a 74-year-old male with a background of ischaemic cardiomyopathy and type 2 diabetes mellitus, was referred to the cardiorespiratory physiotherapy team following a ten-day inpatient admission for decompensated heart failure.

On initial assessment, the patient was noted to have raised jugular venous pressure at 5 cm above the sternal angle and bilateral pitting oedema to the mid-calf, indicating residual fluid overload despite intravenous diuresis. Auscultation of the lung fields revealed persistent bilateral fine inspiratory crackles at both bases. Resting SpO2 was 91% on 2 L/min supplemental oxygen; the patient became acutely hypoxic (SpO2 87%) during a short transfer to the physiotherapy gymnasium, requiring return to the ward and supplemental oxygen titration.

Recent investigations demonstrated a haemoglobin of 94 g/L, consistent with the anaemia of chronic disease, further limiting his functional capacity. Echocardiography performed during admission identified a small pericardial effusion, which the cardiology team deemed haemodynamically insignificant at this stage and recommended monitoring.

Goals of physiotherapy include optimising respiratory function, graded mobilisation within the patient's oxygen tolerance, and patient education regarding symptom monitoring and activity pacing prior to discharge.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When documenting cardiorespiratory patients in referral letters, always include specific values (JVP height, SpO2 percentage, haemoglobin level), the activity context in which symptoms occur (e.g. "during transfer"), and the current management (supplemental oxygen rate, diuretic therapy). This level of detail demonstrates clinical competence and enables the receiving team to plan appropriately.
