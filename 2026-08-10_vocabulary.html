synkinesis
/ˌsɪŋ.kɪˈniː.sɪs/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
神經 Neurological Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
An involuntary muscle movement occurring simultaneously with a deliberate voluntary movement; most commonly seen as a late complication of facial nerve palsy, where aberrant reinnervation causes unintended muscles to contract during intended facial expressions.
繁體中文
協同運動；聯帶運動
Common Collocations

OET Clinical Example
"The patient demonstrated marked oro-ocular synkinesis following Bell's palsy, with involuntary eye closure occurring concurrently with attempted smiling; neuromuscular re-education was commenced to address this aberrant movement pattern."
Related Words
aberrant reinnervation facial nerve palsy synkinetic (adj) associated movements neuromuscular re-education

/ˈhæl.əks/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
骨科 Musculoskeletal
Medical Definition
The first digit of the foot, commonly referred to as the great toe; used in clinical terminology to describe conditions of the big toe, most frequently hallux valgus (lateral deviation causing a bunion deformity) and hallux rigidus (degenerative stiffness of the first metatarsophalangeal joint).
繁體中文

OET Clinical Example

Related Words
bunion (lay term) valgus deformity first metatarsophalangeal joint (1st MTPJ) metatarsalgia plantar fasciitis

decompression
/ˌdiː.kəmˈpreʃ.ən/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
神經 Neurological 骨科 Musculoskeletal Procedures & Interventions
Medical Definition
A surgical procedure to relieve pressure on neural structures — such as the spinal cord or nerve roots — by removing compressive tissue including bone spurs, herniated disc material, or hypertrophied ligamentum flavum; also used in conservative contexts (e.g. traction) to reduce mechanical load on spinal structures.
繁體中文
減壓術；神經減壓
Common Collocations

OET Clinical Example
"Mr. Singh underwent an L4/5 surgical decompression six weeks ago for severe spinal stenosis and has been referred for post-operative physiotherapy to address residual lower limb weakness and to progress his functional mobility within the surgeon's prescribed precautions."
Related Words
laminectomy discectomy foraminotomy spinal stenosis nerve root compression

✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 91)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Mr. Tran, a 58-year-old warehouse supervisor, was referred to the physiotherapy department following surgical decompression at L4/5 for severe lumbar spinal stenosis performed six weeks ago. The neurological sequelae of longstanding neural compression include residual left L5 radiculopathy, diminished sensation over the lateral lower leg, and persisting weakness of foot dorsiflexion and hallux extension (graded 2/5 on manual muscle testing), consistent with incomplete nerve root recovery.

On physical examination, significant bilateral gluteal weakness was identified (left greater than right), contributing to a Trendelenburg gait pattern and reduced pelvic stability on single-leg stance. This is attributed to prolonged pre-operative deconditioning and restricted post-operative mobility. Additionally, the patient demonstrated mild synkinesis of the left lower limb during attempted isolated hip abduction exercises, suggesting incomplete motor re-education following disuse.

Goals of physiotherapy include progressive lumbar stabilisation, targeted gluteal and hip abductor strengthening, neuromotor re-education to address lower limb synkinesis, and a structured return-to-work programme within the surgeon's post-operative precautions.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When writing referral letters for post-surgical patients, always specify the level and type of procedure (e.g. "L4/5 surgical decompression"), the specific neurological sequelae with objective grading (e.g. "hallux extension 2/5"), and the functional impact (e.g. "Trendelenburg gait pattern"). Quantify deficits wherever possible rather than using subjective language.
