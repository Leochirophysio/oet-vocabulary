Word 1 of 5
concentric
/kɒnˈsen.trɪk/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
骨科 Musculoskeletal Procedures & Interventions
Medical Definition
Describing a type of muscle contraction in which the muscle shortens while generating force against a resistance; the predominant contraction type during the lifting phase of resistance exercise and in most functional pushing or pulling movements. The opposite of eccentric contraction, in which the muscle lengthens under load.
繁體中文
向心（收縮）的
Common Collocations
concentric contraction concentric phase concentric loading concentric exercise concentric strength
OET Clinical Example
"Quadriceps strengthening was initiated in the concentric phase only during seated leg press, avoiding eccentric loading in the early post-operative stage to reduce tensile stress across the patellar tendon repair site."
Related Words
eccentric (antonym — muscle lengthens) isometric (no movement) isotonic (constant load) isokinetic (constant speed) muscle contraction (broader term)
Word 2 of 5
isokinetic
/ˌaɪ.səʊ.kɪˈnet.ɪk/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Procedures & Interventions 骨科 Musculoskeletal
Medical Definition
Relating to a form of muscle testing or exercise in which the angular velocity of limb movement is held constant by a dynamometer; regardless of how much force the patient applies, the speed of movement does not change. Isokinetic dynamometry provides objective, quantified data on muscle torque, power, and endurance across the full range of motion.
繁體中文
等速（的）；等動（的）
Common Collocations
isokinetic testing isokinetic dynamometry isokinetic exercise isokinetic strength assessment isokinetic peak torque
OET Clinical Example
"Isokinetic dynamometry at 60°/s revealed a hamstring-to-quadriceps ratio of 0.48 on the right, significantly below the normative threshold of 0.60, indicating relative hamstring deficit and elevated re-injury risk; return to unrestricted sport was not recommended at this stage."
Related Words
isometric (no movement) concentric (shortening) eccentric (lengthening) dynamometer (measuring device) peak torque (key output measure)
Word 3 of 5
splinting
/ˈsplɪn.tɪŋ/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Procedures & Interventions Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
1. (Clinical sign) Voluntary or involuntary muscle guarding that restricts movement to protect a painful area; commonly observed as shallow breathing and reduced chest expansion in patients with rib fractures, pleuritic pain, or post-thoracic surgery. 2. (Procedure) The application of a rigid or semi-rigid device (splint) to immobilise, support, or correct the alignment of a limb or joint.
繁體中文
夾板固定；保護性肌肉固定（胸壁）
Common Collocations
chest splinting respiratory splinting splinting of the wound wrist splinting splinting technique
OET Clinical Example
"The patient demonstrated marked splinting of the right lateral chest wall on day two post-thoracotomy, resulting in reduced tidal volumes and incomplete airway clearance; supported huffing with pillow wound support was introduced to facilitate effective expectoration while minimising pain."
Related Words
splint (the device) immobilisation (broader term) bracing (functional support) pleuritic pain (common cause) serial casting (progressive correction)
Word 4 of 5
tibiofemoral
/ˌtɪb.i.əʊˈfem.ər.əl/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
骨科 Musculoskeletal
Medical Definition
Relating to the articulation between the tibia (shinbone) and the femur (thigh bone); the primary weight-bearing joint of the knee, comprising medial and lateral compartments separated by the menisci. The tibiofemoral joint is frequently affected by osteoarthritis, meniscal pathology, and ligamentous injury, making it a central focus in musculoskeletal physiotherapy.
繁體中文
脛股（的）；膝關節脛股關節面
Common Collocations
tibiofemoral joint medial tibiofemoral compartment tibiofemoral osteoarthritis tibiofemoral alignment tibiofemoral compression
OET Clinical Example
"Radiological assessment confirmed severe medial tibiofemoral compartment narrowing with subchondral sclerosis and marginal osteophyte formation; the patient was referred for physiotherapy to address quadriceps strengthening and gait retraining pending orthopaedic review for possible total knee replacement."
Related Words
patellofemoral (anterior knee compartment) tibia (shinbone) femur (thigh bone) meniscus (intra-articular cushion) osteoarthritis (common pathology)
Word 5 of 5
scapulothoracic
/ˌskæp.jʊ.ləʊ.θəˈræs.ɪk/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
骨科 Musculoskeletal Procedures & Interventions
Medical Definition
Relating to the functional articulation between the scapula (shoulder blade) and the posterior thoracic wall; not a true synovial joint but a physiological gliding interface critical for coordinated shoulder girdle movement. Normal scapulothoracic rhythm — the synchronised upward rotation, posterior tilting, and external rotation of the scapula during arm elevation — is essential for full pain-free shoulder function.
繁體中文
肩胛胸廓（的）
Common Collocations
scapulothoracic rhythm scapulothoracic dyskinesis scapulothoracic muscles scapulothoracic motion scapulothoracic stabilisation
OET Clinical Example
"Assessment revealed disrupted scapulothoracic rhythm on the left, with premature elevation and insufficient upward rotation during active shoulder flexion beyond 90°; a targeted programme of lower trapezius and serratus anterior strengthening was commenced to restore normal scapular kinematics."
Related Words
glenohumeral (true shoulder joint) scapula (the shoulder blade) dyskinesia / dyskinesis (abnormal movement) trapezius (key scapular stabiliser) shoulder girdle (broader complex)
✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 100)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Mr. Jamie Taukiri, a 24-year-old male rugby player, was referred to the physiotherapy department for rehabilitation following a right anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction performed six weeks ago. He is currently 12 weeks post-injury and is progressing through an evidence-based return-to-sport programme.

Objective assessment of knee strength was conducted using isokinetic dynamometry at 60°/s and 180°/s. Results indicated a limb symmetry index of 68% for quadriceps peak torque and 72% for hamstrings at the slower speed, below the 90% threshold required for unrestricted return to competition. Crucially, the hamstring-to-quadriceps ratio was 0.44 on the right, suggesting relative hamstring weakness and an elevated re-injury risk profile.

The rehabilitation programme was progressed to include bilateral leg press with an emphasis on the concentric phase in the initial weeks, with eccentric loading introduced progressively from week eight post-operatively as tolerated. Ongoing strengthening targets the tibiofemoral joint stabilisers, particularly the hamstrings and calf musculature, alongside neuromuscular control activities.

Of note, the patient has also reported intermittent right shoulder discomfort during throwing drills. Clinical assessment revealed altered scapulothoracic rhythm with early elevation and reduced posterior tilt during arm elevation, consistent with scapular dyskinesis secondary to rotator cuff fatigue. A secondary programme addressing lower trapezius and serratus anterior activation was incorporated alongside the knee rehabilitation protocol.

The patient is currently utilising a functional knee brace during all sporting activity; night splinting for tibial rotation correction was trialled for two weeks early post-operatively but was discontinued due to patient discomfort and adequate bony alignment on imaging.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When documenting return-to-sport rehabilitation, always specify objective measures (e.g. limb symmetry index, isokinetic ratios), current functional level, and criteria not yet met for clearance. Linking multiple body-region findings (knee + shoulder) demonstrates thorough physiotherapy assessment in OET writing tasks.
