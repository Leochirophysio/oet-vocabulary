laryngomalacia
/ˌlær.ɪŋ.ɡəʊ.məˈleɪ.ʃiə/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Signs & Symptoms 心肺 Cardiopulmonary
Medical Definition
Congenital softening of the supraglottic laryngeal structures, causing inspiratory stridor and noisy breathing in infants; the most common cause of stridor in neonates, typically resolving spontaneously by 18–24 months of age.
繁體中文
喉軟化症
Common Collocations
congenital laryngomalacia mild laryngomalacia laryngomalacia with feeding difficulties laryngomalacia causing stridor severe laryngomalacia requiring supraglottoplasty
OET Clinical Example
"This six-week-old infant was referred for physiotherapy assessment following a diagnosis of laryngomalacia, presenting with progressive inspiratory stridor, feeding difficulties, and inadequate weight gain requiring urgent multidisciplinary input."
Related Words
tracheomalacia (trachea) bronchomalacia (bronchi) stridor (resulting symptom) supraglottoplasty (surgical fix) laryngomalacic (adj)

Medical Definition

Related Words

Medical Definition
A condition of extreme hyperextension and backward arching of the head, neck, and spine due to severe spasm of the extensor muscles; a serious neurological sign seen in meningitis, tetanus, severe kernicterus, and hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy in neonates.
繁體中文

OET Clinical Example
"On neurological assessment, the neonate exhibited opisthotonus with marked truncal rigidity and intermittent tonic spasms, consistent with the preceding diagnosis of hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy."
Related Words
hypertonia (elevated muscle tone) decerebrate posturing (brainstem) decorticate posturing (cortical) meningism (meningeal irritation) kernicterus (neonatal bilirubin)
Word 4 of 5
stertor
/ˈstɜː.tər/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Signs & Symptoms 心肺 Cardiopulmonary
Medical Definition
A loud, snoring-like respiratory noise produced by partial obstruction at the level of the nasopharynx or oropharynx (upper airway); commonly heard in infants with nasal congestion, adenoidal hypertrophy, or reduced pharyngeal tone; distinct from stridor, which originates at the larynx or trachea.
繁體中文
鼾聲呼吸（上呼吸道阻塞音）
Common Collocations
audible stertor nasal stertor stertor at rest positional stertor stertor and stridor
OET Clinical Example
"The infant presented with persistent audible stertor and mouth breathing at rest, prompting referral for assessment of adenoidal hypertrophy and positioning advice to optimise upper airway patency."
Related Words
stridor (laryngeal/tracheal noise) stertorous (adj) snoring (lay term) upper airway obstruction rhonchi (lower airway secretion sounds)

macroglossia
/ˌmæk.rəˈɡlɒs.i.ə/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical

Medical Definition
Abnormal enlargement of the tongue beyond the confines of the dental arch; may cause feeding difficulties, dysphagia, speech impairment, and upper airway compromise; associated with Down syndrome (trisomy 21), hypothyroidism, and Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome.

Related Words
microglossia (antonym — small tongue) glossoptosis (tongue falling back) ankyloglossia (tongue-tie) Pierre Robin sequence dysphagia (resulting symptom)
✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 95)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Baby Mei, a three-month-old female, was referred to the paediatric physiotherapy service by her neonatologist following a prolonged admission for confirmed pertussis, complicated by frequent apnoeic episodes and oxygen desaturation requiring high-flow oxygen support.

On clinical examination, she demonstrated audible stertor at rest and mild laryngomalacia identified on nasendoscopy, with the combination of upper and lower airway involvement contributing significantly to her feeding difficulties and inadequate weight gain. She was noted to adopt an extended neck posture during feeds, consistent with compensatory airway positioning rather than early opisthotonus.

Physiotherapy aims include optimising secretion clearance via supported positioning and gentle manual techniques, providing structured guidance on feeding positioning to minimise aspiration risk, and educating her parents on signs of respiratory deterioration requiring urgent medical review. Liaison with the neonatal dietitian and speech therapist is recommended to address macroglossia-related feeding concerns, as tongue bulk was noted to impede effective latch and feed efficiency.

📌 OET Writing Tip: In paediatric referral letters, always specify the infant's age in weeks or months, name the causative diagnosis and its complications separately, and clearly outline the multidisciplinary team involved. Avoid using lay terms (e.g. "whooping cough") without first providing the clinical term (e.g. "pertussis").
