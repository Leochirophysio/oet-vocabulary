/ˌɪl.i.əʊˈtɪb.i.əl/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical

Medical Definition
Relating to or connecting the ilium and the tibia; most commonly refers to the iliotibial band (IT band), a thick fascial structure running along the lateral thigh from the iliac crest to the lateral tibial condyle, important for knee stability during running and walking.

✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 89)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Mr. Tane Waititi, a 29-year-old male recreational distance runner, was referred to the physiotherapy department by his general practitioner with a six-week history of right lateral knee pain and bilateral forefoot discomfort.

Biomechanical assessment identified significant lower limb malalignment, including bilateral excessive foot pronation and femoral internal rotation, considered to be the primary contributing factor to his symptoms. Ober's test was positive on the right, consistent with iliotibial band tightness and friction syndrome secondary to the identified malalignment. He also reported bilateral plantar forefoot pain on weight-bearing, consistent with metatarsalgia aggravated by his current weekly training load of 60 kilometres.

On the right, tenderness was localised over the acromioclavicular-equivalent lateral tibial condyle region (Gerdy's tubercle), and pain was reproduced at 30 degrees of knee flexion during the Noble compression test. He is currently restricted to partial weight-bearing jogging and has ceased interval training.

Physiotherapy management will focus on correction of lower limb malalignment through foot orthoses referral and hip abductor strengthening, progressive iliotibial band flexibility work, footwear review to address metatarsalgia, and a graduated return-to-running programme commencing with full weight-bearing walking prior to introduction of running volume.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When writing referral letters for sports injuries, always document the mechanism of injury, current functional limitations (e.g. restricted weight-bearing), and objective clinical findings (e.g. positive Ober's test). Specify the patient's sport/training load as it contextualises symptom severity and informs goal-setting.
