Medical Definition

parkinsonism
/ˈpɑː.kɪn.sə.nɪ.z(ə)m/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
神經 Neurological Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
A clinical syndrome characterised by tremor, bradykinesia, muscle rigidity, and postural instability that resembles Parkinson's disease but arises from causes other than idiopathic Parkinson's, such as medications, vascular changes, or other neurological conditions.
繁體中文
帕金森氏症候群
Common Collocations
drug-induced parkinsonism vascular parkinsonism secondary parkinsonism features of parkinsonism parkinsonism secondary to
OET Clinical Example
"Mr. Thompson was referred for physiotherapy following a diagnosis of drug-induced parkinsonism, presenting with notable bradykinesia, a resting tremor, and a festinating gait pattern that posed a significant falls risk."
Related Words
Parkinson's disease (idiopathic) bradykinesia (slowness of movement) rigidity (muscle stiffness) festinating gait (shuffling, accelerating gait) akinesia (absence of movement)

✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 80)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Mr. David Park, a 62-year-old male with a background of type 2 diabetes mellitus and a recent diagnosis of multiple sclerosis, was referred to the physiotherapy department for comprehensive neurological rehabilitation.

Assessment revealed features consistent with parkinsonism secondary to his neurological condition, including a resting tremor, bradykinesia, and a shuffling gait pattern with reduced step length bilaterally. In addition, he reported a right-sided central scotoma that had been confirmed by ophthalmology, necessitating adaptations to the rehabilitation environment and written patient materials.

Neurological examination also identified signs of distal sensory loss in the lower limbs consistent with diabetic polyneuropathy, which further contributed to his impaired balance and risk of falls. The patient also described intermittent dysphonia, which the treating team attributed to bulbar involvement.

During the initial consultation, the physiotherapist provided clarification regarding each component of the rehabilitation programme, using plain language and teach-back techniques to confirm the patient's understanding of home exercise instructions and safety precautions.

Goals of physiotherapy include improving postural stability, gait re-education, falls prevention education, and ongoing review of the home environment to minimise hazards in light of his visual and sensory deficits.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When describing neurological conditions in a referral letter, always specify aetiology (e.g. "drug-induced parkinsonism", "diabetic polyneuropathy"), the functional impact (e.g. "shuffling gait", "impaired balance"), and any adaptations made (e.g. "adapted written materials"). Linking the diagnosis to specific physiotherapy goals demonstrates clinical reasoning.
