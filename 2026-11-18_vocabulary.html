vasopressor
/ˈveɪ.zəʊˌpres.ə/ noun / adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
心肺 Cardiopulmonary Procedures & Interventions
Medical Definition
An agent that causes vasoconstriction and thereby raises blood pressure; vasopressors are used in intensive care to manage haemodynamic instability, septic shock, and refractory hypotension unresponsive to fluid resuscitation.
繁體中文
升壓藥；血管收縮劑
Common Collocations
vasopressor support vasopressor-dependent initiate vasopressors titrate vasopressors wean vasopressor support
OET Clinical Example
"The patient remained on vasopressor support with noradrenaline at 0.12 mcg/kg/min; physiotherapy input was limited to passive limb mobilisation until haemodynamic stability was achieved."
Related Words
noradrenaline (common vasopressor) inotrope (cardiac contractility agent) vasodilator (antonym) haemodynamic (adj) mean arterial pressure / MAP

transfusion
/trænsˈfjuː.ʒən/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
心肺 Cardiopulmonary Procedures & Interventions
Medical Definition
The direct transfer of blood or blood components — including packed red cells, platelets, or fresh frozen plasma — from a donor into the recipient's circulation, typically to correct anaemia, thrombocytopaenia, coagulopathy, or acute haemorrhage.
繁體中文
輸血；血液輸注
Common Collocations
blood transfusion red cell transfusion platelet transfusion require transfusion post-transfusion reaction
OET Clinical Example
"A two-unit red cell transfusion was administered following a post-operative haemoglobin of 68 g/L; mobilisation was delayed until the transfusion was complete and the patient's vital signs remained stable."
Related Words
autologous transfusion (self-donated) allogeneic (from another donor) haemoglobin (measured before/after) haemorrhage (indication) transfuse (verb)

haemofiltration
/ˌhiː.məʊ.fɪlˈtreɪ.ʃən/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Procedures & Interventions 心肺 Cardiopulmonary
Medical Definition
A form of renal replacement therapy in which blood is pumped through a semipermeable membrane to remove excess fluid, electrolytes, and waste products by convection; commonly used in intensive care for patients with acute kidney injury requiring continuous therapy.
繁體中文
血液過濾；血液濾過
Common Collocations
continuous haemofiltration commence haemofiltration haemofiltration circuit haemofiltration in ICU renal replacement via haemofiltration
OET Clinical Example
"The patient was receiving continuous renal replacement therapy via haemofiltration at the time of physiotherapy review; passive limb exercises and positioning were prioritised to minimise circuit disruption."
Related Words
haemodialysis (similar procedure) renal replacement therapy / RRT acute kidney injury / AKI extracorporeal circuit diuresis (urine-based fluid removal)
✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 104)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style ICU handover context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Mr. David Okafor, a 54-year-old male with a background of type 2 diabetes mellitus and chronic hypertension, was admitted to the intensive care unit following emergency laparotomy for perforated sigmoid diverticulitis complicated by faecal peritonitis.

On day three of admission, he remained on vasopressor support with noradrenaline at 0.15 mcg/kg/min to maintain a mean arterial pressure above 65 mmHg. His morning haemoglobin was 70 g/L; a two-unit red cell transfusion was administered per the surgical team's request prior to commencement of physiotherapy input.

Acute kidney injury (KDIGO stage III) necessitated initiation of continuous renal replacement therapy via haemofiltration on day two. Laryngoscopy performed at the time of intubation confirmed a Cormack–Lehane grade I view, and the patient remained ventilated via an endotracheal tube with FiO₂ 0.5.

Notably, the patient sustained a brief episode of peri-operative asphyxia during induction, requiring emergent bag-mask ventilation and cricoid pressure before successful intubation was achieved.

Physiotherapy goals include ventilator weaning support, passive and active-assisted limb mobilisation to minimise deconditioning, positioning to optimise pulmonary compliance, and airway secretion clearance via closed-circuit suction.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When writing ICU handovers or referrals, always document the patient's current support requirements (e.g. vasopressor dose, ventilator settings), recent interventions (e.g. transfusion, haemofiltration), and any precautions that limit physiotherapy. This tells the receiving therapist exactly what is safe to do and what to avoid.
