anarthria
/æˈnɑː.θri.ə/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
神經 Neurological Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Complete inability to articulate speech due to severe motor dysfunction of the muscles involved in speaking; the most extreme form of dysarthria, in which no intelligible verbal output is produced.
繁體中文
完全構音不能
Common Collocations
severe anarthria complete anarthria anarthria following stroke anarthric patient anarthria in motor neurone disease
OET Clinical Example
"Following a left hemisphere stroke, the patient presented with anarthria and was unable to produce any intelligible verbal output; communication was facilitated via an augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) device."
Related Words
dysarthria (partial — less severe) aphasia (language disorder) anarthric (adj) AAC (augmentative alternative communication) mutism (absence of speech)

alexia
/eɪˈlɛk.si.ə/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
神經 Neurological Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Acquired inability to read words or text following brain injury, despite intact vision and previously normal reading ability; also called word blindness or acquired dyslexia. Distinct from developmental dyslexia.
繁體中文
失讀症
Common Collocations
pure alexia alexia without agraphia alexia with agraphia acquired alexia alexia following occipital infarct
OET Clinical Example
"Neuropsychological screening confirmed pure alexia; the patient was unable to read written text or signage, despite adequate visual acuity and preserved ability to comprehend spoken language and to write from dictation."
Related Words
agraphia (acquired inability to write) aphasia (language disorder) word blindness (lay term) dyslexia (developmental) alexic (adj)
Word 3 of 5
micrographia
/ˌmaɪ.krəˈɡræf.i.ə/ noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
神經 Neurological Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
An abnormality of handwriting characterised by progressively diminishing letter size and increasingly cramped, illegible script; a hallmark feature of Parkinson's disease, resulting from bradykinesia and rigidity of hand muscles.
繁體中文
小字症
Common Collocations
progressive micrographia pronounced micrographia micrographia in Parkinson's disease handwriting assessment revealed micrographia micrographia on fine motor testing
OET Clinical Example
"Handwriting assessment revealed marked micrographia, with letter size diminishing progressively across each line and script becoming illegible by the end of the sentence, consistent with the patient's known diagnosis of Parkinson's disease."
Related Words
bradykinesia (slowness of movement) rigidity (muscle stiffness) graphomotor (relating to handwriting) fine motor impairment Parkinson's disease
Word 4 of 5
postictal
/pɒstˈɪk.t(ə)l/ adjective 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
神經 Neurological Signs & Symptoms
Medical Definition
Relating to or occurring in the period immediately following a seizure; the postictal state is typically characterised by confusion, fatigue, reduced responsiveness, and sometimes transient focal weakness, lasting minutes to hours.
繁體中文
發作後期的（癲癇發作後）
Common Collocations
postictal state postictal confusion postictal drowsiness postictal weakness prolonged postictal period
OET Clinical Example
"Following the generalised tonic-clonic seizure, the patient entered a prolonged postictal state lasting approximately 45 minutes, during which she remained confused, unresponsive to commands, and demonstrated transient left-sided upper limb weakness consistent with Todd's paralysis."
Related Words
ictal (during a seizure) preictal (before a seizure) Todd's paralysis (postictal weakness) convulsion (seizure) epilepsy

advocate
/ˈæd.və.keɪt/ (v) · /ˈæd.və.kɪt/ (n) verb / noun 🔊 Cambridge 📖 M-W Medical
Patient Communication
Medical Definition
To actively support, speak on behalf of, or champion a patient's rights, needs, or interests within the healthcare system; the noun form refers to a person who performs this role. A core professional responsibility in allied health.
繁體中文
倡導、為患者發聲
Common Collocations
advocate for the patient patient advocate advocate on behalf of professional advocacy advocate for early discharge
OET Clinical Example
"As the treating physiotherapist, I have advocated for an urgent home environment assessment prior to discharge, as the patient lives alone and the current layout poses a significant falls risk given her reduced mobility and impaired balance."
Related Words
advocacy (noun) patient rights self-advocacy liaison (communicating between parties) empowerment
✍️ OET Writing Task — Clinical Scenario (Day 101)

Read the scenario below. Notice how today's vocabulary is used in a realistic OET-style referral context. Highlighted words are from today's list.

Mr. David Tran, a 54-year-old male with a background of idiopathic Parkinson's disease (diagnosed six years ago), was referred to your outpatient neurological physiotherapy service for assessment and management of progressive functional decline.

On initial assessment, he demonstrated pronounced micrographia on handwriting tasks, with letter size diminishing markedly across each line and script becoming illegible by the third sentence. He also reported two witnessed seizure episodes over the preceding month — a known comorbidity — with each episode followed by a prolonged postictal state of approximately one hour, during which he was confused and unable to safely mobilise without supervision.

Communication with Mr. Tran was complicated by mild anarthria, with speech difficult to understand at normal conversational pace; a communication aid was used to supplement verbal exchanges during the assessment. Additionally, the patient disclosed difficulty reading therapy handouts, and formal cognitive screening confirmed mild alexia affecting his ability to independently engage with written home exercise programmes.

I have advocated for a multidisciplinary team review, including speech-language therapy and occupational therapy, and have requested an urgent home assessment to evaluate falls risk and optimise the patient's environment ahead of discharge planning.

📌 OET Writing Tip: When a patient has multiple neurological deficits, name each one precisely (e.g. anarthria, not "trouble speaking"; alexia, not "can't read"). Specify the functional impact on therapy participation — this justifies your referral and the services you are requesting.
